Lesson Voice Trial

The concept lessons read their text with the device's own built-in voice. This trial renders real lesson lines with two open-source neural voices on the GPU box, the same way song vocals are made, so you can compare them by ear.

What the test found

324spoken lines across 46 lessons, all fixed text
26 sto render all 22 minutes of lesson speech with Kokoro
9.4 MBfor every line as MP3, per voice
FreeKokoro is Apache-2.0, Chatterbox is MIT; both run offline
  • It's feasible. Because every lesson line is written in advance, each one can be rendered once and shipped as a small audio file, cached on the iPad like the song media. Nothing runs on the iPad except playback, and the voice is the same on every device.
  • Pronunciation needs a short word list. Out of the box, Kokoro reads bass like the fish (55 lines), and can read the note name A as the word "a". Both are fixed here with a pronunciation hint; in the app this would be one shared list (bass, note letters, Italian terms), checked on every line before rendering.
  • Every clip is clear to a speech recogniser. Whisper transcribed all 105 clips: every word was found in every voice (2.6% to 5.8% word differences, nearly all spelling, like "base" and "EDC"). One real slip: Chatterbox added a word ("Well, you did it."). In the app, every rendered line would get this same check, as the song vocals do.
  • Kokoro is steadier; Chatterbox is more expressive but quicker. Chatterbox speaks up to 4.6 words a second on the long lines, against Kokoro's 3.6, and as a larger model it can add or drop words. It needs about 15 s of GPU per minute of speech, against Kokoro's 1 s; both are fine for 330 lines.
  • Checked and correct without help: 4/4 and 3/4 ("four four"), f, p and mf as letters, forte, mezzo forte, Allegro, finger numbers.
